Fasting Blood Sugar vs HbA1c: What Is the Difference?

Two commonly used tests, two different ways of looking at blood glucose. Here is what each one tells you.

By Dr Meet Joshi · MedEduWithDrMeet

1. The basic difference

Fasting plasma glucose (often called fasting blood sugar or FBS) measures the glucose level in your blood at one point in time, after you have fasted. HbA1c measures the percentage of haemoglobin that has glucose attached to it and estimates average blood glucose over roughly the previous 2–3 months.

Remember: Fasting glucose is a snapshot. HbA1c is a longer-term picture.

2. How are the tests performed?

  • Fasting plasma glucose: A blood sample is taken after at least 8 hours without calories. Water is generally allowed. Follow the laboratory or clinician's instructions.
  • HbA1c: A blood sample is taken; fasting is usually not required.

3. Diagnostic ranges

For non-pregnant individuals, commonly used diagnostic categories are:

CategoryFasting plasma glucoseHbA1c
Normal rangeBelow 100 mg/dLBelow 5.7%
Prediabetes range100–125 mg/dL5.7%–6.4%
Diabetes threshold126 mg/dL or higher6.5% or higher

These thresholds are used in recognised diagnostic criteria. In the absence of unequivocal hyperglycaemia, an abnormal result generally needs confirmation with repeat testing or another diagnostic test. Pregnancy has separate testing criteria.

4. Why can the results be different?

  • Glucose can vary with recent food intake, illness, stress, sleep and some medicines.
  • HbA1c reflects a longer period, so a recent change in glucose may not be fully reflected yet.
  • Anaemia, blood loss, transfusion, haemoglobin variants and conditions affecting red blood cell lifespan can make HbA1c less reliable.
  • Some people have a genuine mismatch between HbA1c and measured glucose; a clinician may investigate further.

5. Which test is better?

Neither test is universally better. HbA1c is convenient because fasting is usually unnecessary and it reflects longer-term glucose exposure. Fasting plasma glucose is useful when an HbA1c result may be unreliable or when a direct glucose measurement is preferred. A healthcare professional chooses the test based on the clinical situation.

6. Can one test replace the other?

Not always. They assess glucose in different ways and may identify different people. Depending on the result and clinical context, a clinician may request both tests or confirm an abnormal result with repeat testing.

Key takeaway

Fasting plasma glucose tells you the blood glucose level after an overnight fast; HbA1c estimates average glucose over the previous few months. Understand what each test measures, and do not diagnose yourself from a single result. Discuss results with a qualified healthcare professional.

Medical education disclaimer: This article is for general education only and is not a diagnosis or personal treatment plan. Diagnostic thresholds and test selection may differ in pregnancy and certain medical conditions. Seek advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
