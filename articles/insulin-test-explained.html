Insulin Test Explained: Fasting Insulin, Insulin Resistance & HOMA-IR

Understand what insulin does, what a fasting insulin test can tell you and why no single number tells the whole story.

By Dr Meet Joshi · MedEduWithDrMeet

1. What is insulin?

Insulin is a hormone made by beta cells in the pancreas. After we eat, blood glucose usually rises. Insulin helps the body use and store glucose by acting on tissues such as muscle, fat and liver. It also helps regulate how the body uses fats and proteins.

2. What is a fasting insulin test?

A fasting insulin test measures the amount of insulin in the blood after a period without food. It is commonly collected with a fasting plasma glucose test when a clinician is investigating a particular metabolic or endocrine question.

Fasting instructions can vary by laboratory and by the other tests being ordered. Follow the instructions provided by your healthcare professional or laboratory; do not stop prescribed medicines unless specifically advised.

3. What is insulin resistance?

Insulin resistance means that the body's cells do not respond to insulin as effectively as expected. The pancreas may compensate by producing more insulin to help keep blood glucose within range. Over time, some people develop elevated blood glucose and may progress to prediabetes or type 2 diabetes.

Insulin resistance is associated with several factors, including excess abdominal fat, physical inactivity, genetics, some hormonal conditions and certain medicines. It can also occur in people whose glucose levels are still within the usual range.

Important: A person can have insulin resistance without having diabetes. Diabetes is diagnosed using validated blood glucose or HbA1c criteria—not a fasting insulin result alone.

4. What is HOMA-IR?

HOMA-IR stands for Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance. It is a calculated estimate that uses fasting glucose and fasting insulin. It is a research and clinical-support measure, not a stand-alone diagnostic test for diabetes.

One commonly used calculation when glucose is expressed in mmol/L is:

HOMA-IR = (Fasting insulin in µU/mL × Fasting glucose in mmol/L) ÷ 22.5

When glucose is expressed in mg/dL, a commonly used version is:

HOMA-IR = (Fasting insulin in µU/mL × Fasting glucose in mg/dL) ÷ 405

These are related versions of the same calculation. Use consistent units and the correct insulin units. Do not calculate using a random, non-fasting sample.

5. Is there one normal HOMA-IR cut-off?

No universal cut-off applies to everyone. HOMA-IR values vary with age, population, body composition, clinical setting and the insulin assay used by the laboratory. Research studies may use different thresholds. A value should therefore be interpreted using the local laboratory method and the person's overall clinical picture.

6. Does a high fasting insulin prove insulin resistance?

Not by itself. A higher fasting insulin level may be seen when the body is compensating for reduced insulin sensitivity, but insulin levels are affected by several factors. Assay methods and reference intervals also differ between laboratories. A single result should not be used to label someone as insulin resistant or to make a diagnosis without clinical assessment.

7. Is fasting insulin needed for everyone?

No. Fasting insulin and HOMA-IR are not routine screening tests for everyone and are not required to diagnose prediabetes or diabetes. Depending on the question, a clinician may instead use fasting plasma glucose, HbA1c or an oral glucose tolerance test. These established tests have standard diagnostic criteria.

8. What can help improve insulin sensitivity?

  • Regular physical activity, including walking and muscle-strengthening exercise suited to your ability.
  • A balanced eating pattern with appropriate portions, fibre-rich foods and fewer highly processed foods and sugar-sweetened drinks.
  • Healthy sleep habits and addressing sleep disorders when present.
  • Weight management when medically appropriate, without crash diets or extreme restriction.
  • Not smoking and reviewing medicines or medical conditions with a healthcare professional when relevant.

Key takeaway

Insulin helps regulate blood glucose. Fasting insulin and HOMA-IR can offer additional information in selected situations, but neither is a stand-alone test for diabetes and there is no single HOMA-IR cut-off that fits everyone. Diagnosis and treatment decisions should be based on validated glucose tests and an individual clinical assessment.

Medical education disclaimer: This article is for general education and awareness only. It is not a diagnosis or personal treatment plan. Do not start, stop or change medicines based on an insulin or HOMA-IR result. Discuss your reports with a qualified healthcare professional.
