ANA Test Explained: Antinuclear Antibodies and Autoimmune Diseases

Understand what an ANA test detects, what positive and negative results mean, and why the result alone cannot diagnose an autoimmune disease.

By Dr Meet Joshi · MedEduWithDrMeet

1. What is ANA?

ANA stands for antinuclear antibodies. These are autoantibodies that react with components of the cell nucleus. The immune system normally protects the body from infections, but in some autoimmune conditions it can produce antibodies that react against the body's own cells.

The ANA test looks for these antibodies in a blood sample. It is commonly used as an initial laboratory test when symptoms and clinical findings raise suspicion of certain systemic autoimmune rheumatic diseases.

Remember: ANA is a screening clue—not a diagnosis. A positive ANA does not automatically mean that a person has an autoimmune disease.

2. Why is an ANA test requested?

A clinician may request ANA when a person has features that could suggest a systemic autoimmune condition, such as:

  • Persistent inflammatory joint pain or swelling.
  • Photosensitive rash or other suggestive skin findings.
  • Unexplained mouth or nasal ulcers.
  • Raynaud phenomenon (fingers changing colour with cold or stress).
  • Unexplained features involving more than one organ system, such as joints, skin, kidneys or blood cells.

These symptoms have many possible causes. The decision to test should be based on the overall clinical picture rather than a single non-specific symptom.

3. Which diseases may be associated with a positive ANA?

ANA may be present in several systemic autoimmune diseases, including:

  • Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE).
  • Sjögren syndrome.
  • Systemic sclerosis (scleroderma).
  • Mixed connective tissue disease.
  • Some forms of inflammatory myositis and other connective tissue diseases.

ANA can also be detected in people without these conditions, and may occur in association with some infections, medicines or other clinical circumstances.

4. What does a positive ANA mean?

A positive result means that antinuclear antibodies were detected by the method used. It does not identify a specific disease, establish disease activity or prove that treatment is needed.

The result is interpreted alongside the symptoms, physical examination, titre, staining pattern, other laboratory tests and the patient's medical history.

5. What are ANA titre and dilution?

When ANA is measured by indirect immunofluorescence, the laboratory may report a titre. The sample is diluted in steps and tested to see how far it can be diluted while the staining reaction remains detectable.

For example, a titre of 1:160 means the reaction was still detected at a dilution of one part serum in 160 parts total mixture. Higher titres generally indicate a stronger antibody signal in that assay, but titre alone does not determine whether a person has disease or how severe it is.

6. What are ANA patterns?

With the HEp-2 indirect immunofluorescence method, the appearance of staining may be described using recognised patterns. Examples include:

Pattern (examples)Possible associations—not diagnostic
HomogeneousMay be seen in SLE and some drug-associated autoimmune syndromes
SpeckledCan occur in several connective tissue diseases; interpretation depends on specific antibodies and clinical findings
NucleolarMay be associated with systemic sclerosis and some overlap conditions
CentromereMay be associated with limited cutaneous systemic sclerosis
Peripheral / rimMay be reported in some settings; interpretation depends on method and clinical context

Patterns are clues, not disease labels. Reporting terminology and interpretation may vary between laboratories. Some laboratories use the International Consensus on ANA Patterns (ICAP) classification.

7. What does a negative ANA mean?

A negative result makes some ANA-associated systemic autoimmune diseases less likely, especially when a suitable sensitive method is used. However, it does not exclude every autoimmune condition. Some diseases may be ANA-negative, and conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis are not diagnosed using ANA as the primary test.

8. What are ENA and anti-dsDNA tests?

If clinical suspicion remains or ANA is positive, a clinician may request more specific antibody tests based on the suspected disease. Examples include:

  • Anti-dsDNA: Can support the evaluation of SLE in the appropriate clinical setting.
  • Anti-Sm: Relatively specific for SLE, although not present in every patient.
  • Anti-SSA/Ro and anti-SSB/La: May be associated with Sjögren syndrome and SLE.
  • Anti-centromere and anti-topoisomerase I (Scl-70): May help evaluate systemic sclerosis in the appropriate context.

These tests are selected according to symptoms and clinical assessment; ordering a large panel without a clear reason can produce confusing results.

9. Can healthy people have a positive ANA?

Yes. Positive ANA results can occur in otherwise healthy people, and the likelihood of a clinically meaningful result depends on the titre, test method, age, sex, background prevalence and symptoms. This is one reason ANA testing is not recommended as a general screening test for people without suggestive clinical features.

10. Can medicines cause a positive ANA?

Some medicines can be associated with ANA positivity or drug-induced lupus-like illness. The significance depends on the specific medicine, duration of use, symptoms and other tests. Do not stop a prescribed medicine on your own; discuss concerns with the prescribing clinician.

Key takeaway

ANA testing can help clinicians investigate suspected systemic autoimmune disease, but a positive result is not a diagnosis and a negative result does not exclude every autoimmune condition. The titre, pattern, specific antibody tests and—most importantly—the clinical picture must be considered together.

Medical education disclaimer: This article is for general education and awareness only. It is not a diagnosis or personal treatment plan. Do not diagnose an autoimmune disease or change medicines based on an ANA report alone. Discuss results with a qualified healthcare professional.
